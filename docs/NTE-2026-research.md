# FIRST Global Challenge 2026 — New Technology Experience (NTE) Research Dossier

Compiled 2026-07-30. Primary source: the official *2026 FIRST Global Challenge – Igniting Innovation New Technology Experience Guide* (released 17 June 2026, v1, no amendments yet).

---

## 0. TL;DR — the things that actually decide outcomes

1. **Phase 1 closes 3 August 2026, 11:59 PM UTC.** No late submissions, no edits. That is ~4 days from today.
2. Phase 1 is judged on **two separate artifacts**: a **≤2-minute video** and a **static portrait display advertisement**. They are scored by different panels against different rubrics and award separately.
3. **Only 15 teams** survive Phase 1 (was 10 last year). **Only 5** present on-site. **Top 3** pitch on the main stage.
4. The rubric rewards *local, specific, evidenced* problems and *implemented* solutions — "status of your solution / actions you have taken" is a **required** video component.
5. A website is **not** an NTE deliverable. It earns points only as (a) proof of actions taken, (b) the demo vehicle, (c) the display ad's call-to-action, (d) awareness-building evidence in Phase 3.

## 1. What the NTE is

The New Technology Experience is FIRST Global's non-robotics competition track, running in parallel with the Robotics Challenge. Introduced 2021. Each year it mirrors the FGC theme and asks teams to research and develop a real technological solution to a global problem, framed through their own community or nation.

Official framing: "provides students with the opportunity to become experts in emerging fields, establishes stronger network connections among FIRST Global teams, and improves the participants' competitiveness for college, internships, and employment."

Participation is **optional** and open to all registered 2026 FIRST Global teams. Teams are encouraged to bring NTE project materials to Incheon to display in their pits (regardless of whether they advance).

Rules questions go to **support@first.global**. Only FIRST Global answers count as official clarification. Amendments are logged in the Updates table on the guide's cover page — check it periodically.

## 3. The 2026 NTE structure

Three phases (2025 had only two — this is the biggest structural change).

| Date | Milestone |
|---|---|
| 17 June | NTE announced |
| **3 August** | **Phase 1 submissions due (11:59 PM UTC)** |
| 17 August | Semifinalists announced (top 15) |
| **7 September** | **Phase 2 submissions due (11:59 PM UTC)** |
| 14 September | Finalists announced (top 5) |
| 7 October | Phase 3 presentations to judges, Incheon |
| 10 October | Top three main-stage pitches + awards |
| Ongoing | Project continuation |

### Funnel
- Phase 1 (Qualification, 17 Jun – 3 Aug): open to all → **15 Semifinalists**
- Phase 2 (Semifinalist, 17 Aug – 7 Sep): refinement + demo plan + continuation plan → **5 Finalists**
- Phase 3 (Finalist, 14 Sep – 10 Oct): live 15-minute presentation in Incheon → **Top 3**
- Top 3 give a 90-second elevator pitch on the main stage on 10 October

### Awards
- All Phase 1 submitters: **printed participation certificates** for student members
- Top 3 **display advertisements**: digital recognition certificates (separate award track)
- Phase 2 Semifinalists and Phase 3 Finalists: digital recognition certificates
- 1st / 2nd / 3rd overall: **FIRST Global Innovator Award in honor of Fred Smith**, presented at Incheon
- All display advertisements are showcased by FIRST Global at the event

Mentorship is offered ("teams may have the option to work with a mentor") in Phase 2 and Phase 3.

## 4. The three categories — pick exactly one

The guide requires teams to **select one** of Detect / Combat / Prevent as the project focus and to state it explicitly in the video.

### 4.1 Detect
Identifying fires in natural environments as early as possible to enable rapid response and containment. Early detection is critical because wildfires spread with extraordinary speed, threatening lives, wildlife, property and ecosystems while releasing large amounts of CO₂. Extreme climate conditions have raised risk and frequency, making reliable detection more important than ever.

Guide's further reading: WIPO Green Technology Book (Wildfire chapter); ASME Open Journal of Engineering, "A Review of Technologies for the Early Detection of Wildfires"; Net Zero Insights, "Key Technologies Driving Effective Wildfire Management".

### 4.2 Combat
Strategies and technologies to suppress, contain and extinguish fires once detected, plus fuel-load reduction before ignition. Even an early-detected fire can outpace response. Tools must evolve as fires grow larger and more intense.

Guide's further reading: Discovery, "Wildfire Technology: Tackling the Spread of Wilderness Fires with Digital Tech"; Forbes, "AI-Driven Innovations In Wildfire Management"; German Aerospace Center (DLR), "Fighting Wildfires with Cutting-Edge Technology".

### 4.3 Prevent
Proactive measures reducing the likelihood of ignition and spread — targeting the conditions and human factors that make landscapes vulnerable before any fire starts. Described in the guide as "arguably the most cost-effective phase of wildfire management."

Guide's further reading: WWF, "Fires, Forests, and the Future: A Crisis Raging Out of Control?"; World Economic Forum, "How Investing in Wildfire Resilience Protects Communities, Forests and Markets"; UN, "As Wildfires Increase, Integrated Strategies for Forests, Climate and Sustainability Are Ever More Urgent".

### Technologies the guide names as relevant
Monitoring and sensing · machine learning · artificial intelligence · mathematical and statistical modeling and mapping · communication systems · robotics. (Explicitly "not limited to".)

## 5. PHASE 1 — exact requirements

### 5.1 Submission rules
- **One** video and **one** display advertisement per team. No more.
- No copyrighted elements without consent.
- Submitted via the official form linked in the guide, by **11:59 PM UTC on 3 August 2026**.
- No late submissions. No edits after submission.

### 5.2 Video — format requirements (hard rules)
| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Language | English subtitles required if spoken language is not English |
| Duration | **Maximum 2 minutes** — content beyond 2:00 will not be reviewed |
| File title | `Team ___ NTE` (e.g. `Team Spain NTE`) |
| File type | `.mp4` |
| Resolution | 1920×1080, 30 fps |
| Overlays | None required |
| Music | Optional; if used must be from hooksounds.com or a Creative Commons source |

Allowed structures: spoken narrative, interview, slide show, photo montage, solution demonstration, stitched clips, screen recording, animation, or other media. **Explicitly banned: speeding up audio to fit the time limit.** Speak at a normal pace.

Resources the guide points to: *Team Profile Video Best Practices* document; free animation resources at https://first.global/resources/ under "Digital Animation".

### 5.3 Video — required content components
Every one of these must appear, in audio or visually:

| Required component | Guide's prompts |
|---|---|
| FIRST Global team name | e.g. "FIRST Global Team Spain" |
| Project title | Should describe problem and/or solution, **10 words or less** — e.g. "Detecting Wildfires Using Artificial Intelligence" |
| Category selected | Detect, Combat, or Prevent |
| Problem statement in the context of your community or nation | Brief description of the problem, **where** it is occurring, **who** is affected, and the **magnitude** |
| Reason you chose this topic | Why this problem? What evidence do you see of it? What are current and future risks in your category? |
| Solution description and plan | What is the solution? What technology does it use? How does it address the problem? What actions will you take? How will it impact your community? |
| Status of your solution | Have you taken actions to implement it — which? If not started, do you have plans? What has to happen first? |

Optional additions the guide invites:
- Demonstrate or simulate the solution
- Additional background information and data supporting the project
- The team's plans to continue the project after FGC 2026

### 5.4 Display advertisement — format requirements (hard rules)
| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Type | Static image only — no dynamic features |
| Language | English required |
| Size | **2480 × 3508 px, portrait** (A4 @ 300 dpi). Landscape is non-compliant. |
| Overlay | Must include the **horizontal FIRST Global logo** and the **official team name** at the **TOP** |
| File title | `Team ___ NTE` |
| File type | `.jpg` / `.jpeg` |

Intent per the guide: "a way to visually market and promote your project to an **unfamiliar audience** across digital and printed platforms. Teams should think like an advertiser trying to get people to buy your product or service. Use eye-catching visuals, concise text, and a compelling **call-to-action**."

Tooling suggested: Canva, Adobe Express, PowerPoint, or a pre-built template. Appendix A of the guide has design guidelines and examples. Resource links given: Canva advertising poster templates, Behance for inspiration.

### 5.5 PHASE 1 JUDGING RUBRIC — verbatim

Judged by "a panel of subject matter experts and thought leaders."

**Videos** (panel selects 15 Semifinalists). Ranked on:

| Attribute | Judge's question |
|---|---|
| **CONCEPT** | Did the team clearly state the selected category? Did the team provide a clear problem statement? Did the team explain why they chose their project? Did the team provide a solution and their plan to address the problem? |
| **CREATIVITY** | Is the team's proposed solution original or creative? |
| **IMPACT** | What is the magnitude of potential impact of the project? Will the team's solution make small or large improvements to the problem? Is the solution scalable for expanded application or distribution? |
| **TECHNICAL** | Is the solution feasible and/or is there a realistic path to implementation? Does the team show they learned about the engineering and science associated with the category, problem, and solution? |
| **QUALITY** | Was thought and effort put into the project? Were the explanations thorough? |
| **VISUAL IMPACT** | Does the video use compelling visuals to enhance the story? Do the visuals effectively support and elevate the team's message? |
| **PROCEDURAL** | Did the team adhere to the submission content and requirement guidelines? Does the project align with one of the categories and does it address a specific technology related to wildfires within the category? |

**Display Advertisements** (panel selects top 3 for an award; all are showcased at Incheon). Ranked on:

| Attribute | Judge's question |
|---|---|
| **DESIGN EFFECTIVENESS** | Is it clear the team was intentional in combining visuals, text, and a call-to-action in the display advertisement to pitch their product or service? |
| **PROCEDURAL** | Did the team adhere to the display advertisement content and requirement guidelines? |

> No numeric weights or point values are published. Attributes appear equally weighted. There is no published tiebreak procedure.

## 6. PHASE 2 — Semifinalist round (17 Aug – 7 Sep)

The 15 Semifinalists "further develop their idea, devise a way to demonstrate their solution, and present a plan for continuing work on the project following" FGC 2026.

**Required submission content:**
- Changes to the project since the Phase 1 video submission
- How the solution would be **demonstrated at FGC 2026 in Incheon**
- The plan to **continue the project after** FGC 2026

Deadline 11:59 PM UTC, 7 September 2026, via a form ("link coming soon" as of guide v1). No late submissions or edits.

**Judging criteria: "Details to be provided."** — Not published in guide v1. Watch the Updates table. Given the required content, expect criteria mapped to: quality of iteration/refinement, credibility and impressiveness of the live demonstration plan, and the seriousness/realism of the continuation plan.

Mentorship available during this phase.

## 7. PHASE 3 — Finalist round (14 Sep – 10 Oct)

Five Finalist teams present live in Incheon (or via live video feed if unable to travel) on **Wednesday, 7 October 2026**. Times announced beforehand. Open to all teams, guests and spectators.

**Presentation must include:**
- A 90-second overview / elevator pitch
- Project title, category selected, and reason the project was selected
- Problem statement and solution description
- **Actions taken** to implement the solution
- Plan to continue work following the event
- Visual aids (video, slides, pictures)
- Demonstrate or simulate the solution
- **How you have built awareness in your community and/or nation and inspired others to take action** on wildfires

**Timing structure — strictly 15 minutes total:**
- 0:00–1:30 — elevator pitch ("a brief, persuasive speech that clearly explains an idea... The goal is to spark interest and leave the listener wanting to know more")
- 1:30–10:00 — detailed presentation (8 min 30 s)
- 10:00–15:00 — judges' questions (5 min)

**Logistics:** podium in the room; screen connectable to a laptop; audio available for video. No limit on number of student presenters. Presenters need not be on the official traveling team but **must be registered team members**.

**Safety:** teams may not demonstrate any potentially dangerous device. If in doubt, email support@first.global with the intended plan in advance to confirm it meets venue requirements. (Relevant here — this is a *fire* theme. Do not plan open-flame demos.)

**Main stage:** top three judged teams give their 90-second pitch in front of spectators on 10 October 2026.

## 9. What has historically won — Innovator Award podiums

| Year | Gold | Silver | Bronze |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 (Carbon, w/ XPRIZE) | **Zimbabwe** — bioplastic from cassava starch | **Indonesia** — enhanced vegetative propagation of a carbon-consuming endangered native tree | **Greece** — more efficient heating/cooling for resort hotels |
| 2023 (Energy Evolution) | **Lebanon** — solar and hydroponics farm | **Moldova** — water cooling system to improve solar panel efficiency | **Tunisia** — converting seagrass into biogas |
| 2024 (Feeding the Future) | **Spain** — *SUNHARVEST*, integrating agrivoltaics and water management | **Ireland** — replacing chemical fertilizers with electro-farming | **Iran** — *HydroFusion*, integrated closed-loop agricultural system |
| 2025 (Eco Equilibrium) | **UAE** — "Semi-Permeable Sodium Alginate Hydrogels for Cell and Genetic Preservation" | **Iran** | **Colombia** |

2025 Gold quote (UAE): *"I think we can democratize cell preservation for everyone and make it accessible for anyone in any part of the world."*

### Inferred winning pattern (my analysis, not official)

1. **Hyper-local problem, globally scalable solution.** Every winner picked something visibly true of their own country — Zimbabwe's cassava, Tunisia's seagrass, Greece's tourism sector, Spain's dryland agriculture — then argued it generalizes. The rubric's IMPACT line ("scalable for expanded application or distribution") rewards exactly this two-step.
2. **Applied science / physical prototype beats a pure app.** The podium skews heavily to materials, chemistry, biology and hardware systems. Software-only projects are common submissions but rare on the podium. A software or AI project *can* win — the guide names ML/AI/modeling explicitly — but it needs real deployed data, real users, and a tangible demo to compete on TECHNICAL and on the Phase 3 "demonstrate or simulate" requirement.
3. **Cheap inputs, especially waste streams or abundant local materials.** Cassava starch, seagrass, water cooling. Reads as feasible *and* equitable.
4. **Named and branded.** SUNHARVEST, HydroFusion. With the display-ad award added in 2026, branding is now directly scored.
5. **Integration over invention.** Winners often combine two known technologies into a new system (solar + hydroponics; agrivoltaics + water management) rather than inventing from nothing. Scores well on CREATIVITY without failing TECHNICAL feasibility.
6. **Accessibility framing.** "Democratize… accessible for anyone in any part of the world." Judges are development-and-impact people, not just engineers.
7. **Evidence of having actually done something.** "Status of your solution" is required in Phase 1 and "actions taken" is required in Phase 3. A team that built and tested anything, however small, outranks a team with a beautiful concept.

### Category-choice consideration for 2026
No public data yet on 2026 submissions. Expect **Detect** to be the most crowded category — camera + AI early-detection is the obvious first idea and will be heavily duplicated across 190+ teams. **Prevent** is the category the guide itself argues is most cost-effective, and is the least obvious to a high-school team — likely the best differentiation-per-effort. **Combat** carries a safety constraint on live demos.

## 10. Where a website fits

A website is **not a required or scored NTE deliverable at any phase**. It earns its keep in four specific ways:

1. **Phase 1, "Status of your solution"** — a live, working site is concrete evidence that the team took action rather than only planned. Shown on screen in the video, it reads as real.
2. **Phase 1, VISUAL IMPACT + QUALITY** — screen-recorded footage of a real interface is far stronger B-roll than stock imagery.
3. **Display advertisement call-to-action** — the ad rubric explicitly rewards a compelling call-to-action. A short URL is the natural one, and the site is where that CTA lands. The ad is printed and displayed physically at Incheon, so a memorable domain matters.
4. **Phase 2 "how the solution would be demonstrated at Incheon" and Phase 3 "demonstrate or simulate the solution" + "how you built awareness in your community and/or nation."** A public site with real traffic, sign-ups, partner logos, or reported data is the cleanest awareness evidence available, and a browser-based demo is safe where a fire demo is not.

Design implications that follow from the above:
- Must survive being **screen-recorded at 1920×1080** and read clearly in a 2-minute video.
- Must work from a **printed poster CTA** — short URL, no deep link required to get the point.
- Should surface **evidence and numbers** (data, users, area covered, partners) since IMPACT and TECHNICAL are what judges score.
- Should carry the **project brand** consistently with the display ad, since both are judged by the same event audience.
- Should still be live and updated for Phase 2/3 — continuation is scored.
